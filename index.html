Disaster safety, first aid & traditional knowledge

Ayurveda is a traditional system of health practices originating in India. The word comes from Sanskrit: Ayur (life) + Veda (knowledge).

It is based on the belief that health and wellness depend on a balance between mind, body, and spirit. Unlike modern medicine which focuses on treating disease, Ayurveda focuses on preventing it.

🌍 Ayurveda is recognized by the World Health Organization (WHO) as a traditional system of medicine.

Ayurveda identifies three biological energies called Doshas that govern our body and mind:

  • Vata (Air + Space): Controls movement, breathing, circulation. When imbalanced: anxiety, dry skin, constipation.
  • Pitta (Fire + Water): Controls digestion, metabolism, intelligence. When imbalanced: anger, inflammation, acidity.
  • Kapha (Earth + Water): Controls structure, immunity, stability. When imbalanced: weight gain, congestion, laziness.
Everyone has a unique mix of all three doshas — knowing yours helps maintain balance through diet, herbs, and lifestyle.

Researchers study compounds found in traditional plants. A finding about one compound does not prove that a home remedy treats an illness.

  • Turmeric (Curcumin): Studied for possible anti-inflammatory effects; evidence about benefits in people varies by use and preparation.
  • Tulsi: Used traditionally; reliable evidence for treating illness is limited.
  • Ginger: Some evidence suggests ginger may ease some types of nausea; effects vary.
  • Neem: Used in some personal-care products; this does not establish it as a treatment for illness.
💡 Ayurveda complements — but does not replace — modern medicine. Always see a doctor for serious conditions.

Kerala is globally recognized as the heartland of authentic Ayurvedic practice. The state's tropical climate, heavy rainfall, and rich biodiversity make it ideal for growing medicinal plants.

  • Kerala has over 900+ Ayurvedic hospitals and thousands of traditional practitioners (Vaidyas).
  • The Ashtangahridayam, a major Ayurvedic text, was written in Kerala by Vagbhata.
  • Kerala's Panchakarma treatments attract visitors from around the world.
🏡 Many Kerala households still use home remedies — turmeric milk, tulsi tea, ginger for digestion — passed down through generations.

Some people use familiar, low-risk traditional practices for comfort with mild, non-urgent symptoms. They are not proven substitutes for medicine or medical care.

  • ✅ Comfort only: familiar food or drink practices for mild symptoms, if safe for you; never delay needed care
  • ❌ See a doctor for: Deep wounds, high fever (above 102°F), severe allergic reaction, chest pain, difficulty breathing, symptoms lasting more than 3 days
⚕️ AyurAid is a first aid guide. It does not replace professional medical advice.

Herbs contain different compounds and are studied in different ways. This section is for learning only; it does not establish that a herb treats an emergency or illness:

  • Turmeric → Curcumin: Studied for possible biological effects; not proof that turmeric treats infection or injury
  • Ginger → Gingerols & Shogaols: Studied for possible effects on nausea; it may not help everyone
  • Tulsi → Eugenol: Traditional use does not prove that tulsi treats fever or infection
  • Aloe → Aloin & Acemannan: Some products are used for skin comfort; do not apply plant gel to serious burns or wounds
  • Neem → Nimbin: Traditional use does not establish neem as treatment for infection

Before: Follow local alerts, identify higher ground and evacuation routes, charge phones, and protect medicines and documents in waterproof bags.

During: Evacuate when instructed. Never walk, swim or drive through floodwater. Keep away from bridges, drains and fallen wires.

After: Return only after an official all-clear. Avoid contaminated water and clean hands and wounds safely.

Track official IMD and local authority alerts. Secure loose outdoor items, keep water and medicines ready, and know your nearest safe shelter. During the storm, stay inside a sturdy building away from windows and follow evacuation instructions. Do not go outside during a brief calm. Afterward, avoid damaged buildings and downed power lines.

Rest in shade or a cool place, reduce strenuous activity in peak heat, and drink water regularly unless medically restricted. Check on children, older adults and outdoor workers. Confusion, fainting or very hot skin needs urgent help: call 112 and begin cooling while help is coming. For any first aid, check scene safety first and do not put herbal pastes or powders on wounds or burns.

Traditional knowledge and safe-use notes

How to Use
Turmeric is used traditionally in food and wellness practices; it is not a substitute for medicine or first aid. Do not apply powder, oil, or paste to cuts, wounds, eyes, or burns. Use clean water and sterile dressings for first aid.
  1. Traditional food use is for learning, not emergency treatment
  2. Do not put turmeric powder, oil, or paste on wounds or burns
  3. For a small wound, rinse with clean water
  4. Cover with a sterile dressing
  5. Seek medical help for serious injury or signs of infection
⚠️ Do not put turmeric on any wound or burn. For bleeding that will not stop, seek urgent medical help.

Traditional skin care 😤 Pimples / Acne
How to Use
Neem has traditional uses, but homemade leaf water is not sterile and is not a proven treatment. Do not use it on wounds or infected skin; use clean running water for minor wound care and seek medical advice for worsening symptoms.
  1. Neem is described here as traditional knowledge, not treatment
  2. Do not put homemade leaf water on wounds or infected skin
  3. Use clean running water for minor wound care
  4. Follow the label on products intended for skin
  5. Get medical advice if symptoms worsen
⚠️ Do not put homemade neem preparations on wounds or use them to treat infection.
Used For
😷 Cough & Cold Traditional comfort 🧠 Stress relief
How to Use
Some people use tulsi tea as a traditional comfort drink for a mild cough. Evidence is limited; it does not treat infection, fever, or breathing problems. A warm drink may feel soothing if safe for you.
  1. A warm drink may feel soothing for a mild cough
  2. Use a familiar food-strength preparation only if safe for you
  3. Do not use it instead of prescribed medicine or medical care
  4. Ask a health worker before herbal products for children, pregnancy, or regular medicines
  5. Seek help for breathing difficulty, chest pain, high fever, or worsening symptoms
⚠️ Traditional use is not proven treatment. Ask a health worker before using herbal products during pregnancy or with regular medicines.
Used For
Traditional skin use
How to Use
Aloe is used traditionally for skin comfort, but a plant remedy is not burn treatment. Cool a burn under clean, cool running water; do not apply plant gel, oils, or pastes to a serious burn.
  1. Aloe is described here as traditional knowledge, not emergency treatment
  2. Do not apply plant gel to serious burns or open wounds
  3. Cool a burn with clean, cool running water
  4. Cover loosely with a clean non-stick dressing
  5. Get medical care for serious or worsening burns
⚠️ Do not put plant gel on open wounds or serious burns. Cool burns with clean, cool running water and get help when needed.
Used For
🤢 Nausea Traditional food use
How to Use
Ginger is traditionally used as a food or warm drink for mild nausea; it may not help everyone and is not a treatment for serious illness.
  1. Cut a 1-inch piece of fresh ginger
  2. Chew directly with a pinch of rock salt for quick relief, OR
  3. Boil sliced ginger in 1 cup of water for 5 minutes
  4. Strain, add honey or lemon if preferred
  5. Sip warm slowly
⚠️ Ask a health worker before herbal products if pregnant or taking regular medicines.
Used For
💇 Hair Loss 👁️ Eye Health 🩸 Blood Sugar 🤢 Mild Diarrhoea
How to Use
Curry leaves are a familiar food ingredient. They are not a treatment for diarrhoea or nausea; prioritize safe fluids and seek care if symptoms are severe or persistent.
  1. Take 10–15 fresh curry leaves
  2. Wash them well under clean water
  3. Chew directly on an empty stomach in the morning, OR
  4. Boil in 1 cup of water for 5 minutes
  5. Strain and sip warm
⚠️ Use fresh leaves only. Dried leaves have reduced potency.
